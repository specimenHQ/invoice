# INVOICE - BOOK 1

Panel map

## Formal Rule

Book 1 has 313 panels.

Pages 1-51 use six panels per page. Six panels echo the six faces of the cube and the six enclosing faces of the wooden crate.

Page 52 uses seven panels. The seventh panel is the first formal breach into Book 2. Outside the cube/crate system, the page language can become seven.

If the narrator returns to the crate/cube logic later, the page system can return to six.

## Scope Rule

Book 1 is only the process of getting out of the first crate and reaching the door.

Do not include the warehouse/memory-opening arc in Book 1.

Do not include eggs, helpful memories, hurtful memories, befriending memories, digging, or building a home from crate wood as events in Book 1. Those belong to Book 2 and later.

## Material Rule

The first prison is a wooden crate in a dark room that represents the family home.

The family-home room is symbolic, not necessarily literal domestic scenery. It is the first place we go as babies and the first place we break out of as teens.

The actual Cube is stone, otherworldly, and humming.

The narrator is growing too large inside the crate. The crate mimics the womb and then the family home: protection becoming pressure, pressure becoming boundary, boundary becoming break.

Tree/Wood/Egg rule: the Tree does not make the egg. The egg comes from the narrator: his memories and inner self. The Tree makes the wood that houses the egg. The Tree represents society, tradition, inherited support, and answers paid for by the mistakes of those before us. It is powerful and useful, but it hides the truth by turning inherited answers into containers.

Thesis: it is our job to break the crates. Break the container when it stops protecting life and starts preventing growth.

Geometric emergence: dot, line, box, cube, growing grid. The opening should show dimension arriving before material. Wood comes later.

## Future Notes

Book 2 reversal to preserve: the narrator returns to what was left behind, like a molt. Empty crates eventually become building material for a home inside the Cube. Later, befriended memories help him build with the leftover crate wood.

Memory design for later: memories are children, or the essence of childhood. They are cute, small, and helpful at the core. If they seem frightening, it is because they have been trapped or damaged.

Crate-opening gamble for later: a crate may contain an egg, a helpful memory, or a hurtful memory. Hurtful memories must be caught, held, contained, calmed, or befriended. Destroying them damages the fabric of the whole system.

## Page System

Pages 1-3: point/dot, empty blackness, hidden crate interior, no body.

Pages 4-6: line, punctuation as pressure against unseen walls.

Pages 7-9: box, first body sound as pain/vibration, no anatomy.

Pages 10-14: cube, letters as cube geometry, not yet stable words.

Pages 15-18: slow-growing grid, repeated cube-logic without explicit cubes or warehouse.

Pages 19-22: growth pressure, planes and corners become readable.

Pages 23-26: wood grain appears from blackness.

Pages 27-30: upper door seam appears.

Pages 31-34: sliver of light enters through the seam.

Pages 35-38: the crate breaks.

Pages 39-42: dark symbolic family-home space outside the crate.

Pages 43-45: follow the sliver/line through grey-dark space.

Pages 46-48: humming door, stone/Cube-adjacent boundary.

Pages 49-51: threshold, escaped crate, not yet beyond the door.

Page 52: first silhouette, first paragraph, first seventh panel.

## Rendering Medium (ties to Language Ladder)

The narrator's body is rendered, not drawn: it is built from the same marks the Language Ladder is climbing, panel by panel, page by page. Pages 1-3 through the letters stage use sparse marks/low-density ASCII with no coherent shape. As the ladder climbs through object-fragments and the grid, character density increases and a shape starts to cohere without being nameable.

Immediately before Page 52, the density character set narrows to strictly 0 and 1: the body is legible as binary mass before it is legible as flesh. Page 52's seventh panel is a hard cut, not a fade, from binary rendering to a real photograph (drawn from the reference-image set), gated behind a full white rupture frame and the book's strongest haptic pulse. Everything up to that cut is ASCII/binary; everything after is real.
