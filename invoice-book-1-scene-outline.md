# INVOICE - BOOK 1

Scene-by-scene graphic outline

## Core Rule

Book 1 is the birth of readability.

The whole book is the struggle to get out of the first crate and reach the door.

Nothing from the warehouse/memory-opening arc happens in Book 1. Eggs, helpful memories, hurtful memories, crate gambling, befriending memories, digging, and building a home from crate wood belong to Book 2 and later.

Book 1 is struggle in darkness.

The visual world moves from abstract, surreal, and random toward order, clarity, and grey realism. Language follows the same path. It begins with marks, then punctuation, then body sounds, then letters, then broken clusters, then small words, then fragments. The first full paragraph arrives only on Page 52.

No normal prose until the final page.

The main space of Book 1 is the inside of a wooden crate. The narrator does not know this. The reader should not fully know this at first. The crate is the first cube: womb, family home, prison, room, page, panel, and meta-structure.

The dark room outside the crate represents the family home. It is symbolic, not necessarily literal domestic scenery. It is the first place we go as babies and the first place we break out of as teens.

The final page may reveal the narrator only as a silhouette. The body remains withheld until then.

## Language Ladder

1. Marks only
2. Punctuation
3. Body sound
4. Numbers / counting
5. Single letters
6. Broken letter clusters
7. First stable shape-word
8. Slow-growing grid
9. Object/action words
10. Fragment chains
11. Binary density (literal 0s and 1s form the silhouette)
12. First paragraph

## Medium Rule (Digital / ASCII / Binary)

The narrator is rendered in ASCII, not as a stylistic choice but as a diegetic fact: before he has a body, he has marks, and marks are not real in the same sense a photograph is real. He is blind, in the dark, unresolved. The medium tells the truth about his state.

The body itself is built the same way the language builds: the period on Page 1 is the seed of the eventual silhouette. As the Language Ladder climbs, the character-density rendering of the body should tighten in step with it. Early pages use sparse marks and low-density characters. As letters and object-fragments arrive, density increases and shapes start to cohere without being nameable yet.

Immediately before the Page 52 threshold, the density character set narrows to only 0 and 1. The body is legible as binary mass before it is legible as flesh: he is data before he is a person. This is the last rung before the paragraph and the rupture.

The Page 52 transition (seventh panel, first full paragraph, the door) is a hard cut, not a fade. Binary does not dissolve into flesh, it converts. A full white rupture frame separates the last binary-rendered panel from the first real photograph, paired with the strongest haptic pulse in the book. Everything before the cut is ASCII/binary; everything after is real photographic material pulled from the reference-image set.

## Cube / Crate Grammar

The crate is the visual and metaphysical engine of Book 1.

All crates are wooden. The first prison is a wooden crate in a symbolic family-home darkness.

The actual Cube is different from the crates: it is stone, otherworldly, and humming. Book 1 may let the stone Cube be felt as a low hum or distant pressure, but it should not explain the Cube.

The pain comes from growing too large inside the crate. The crate mimics the womb and then the family home: first protection, then pressure, then boundary, then break. It does not expand enough. Pressure, pain, and sound are the first signs of growth.

Use cube mathematics quietly:

1. Six faces: wall, floor, ceiling, lid, door, page, panel.
2. Twelve edges: slashes, pressure lines, cracks, gutters, seams.
3. Eight corners: pain points, awareness points, trapped perspective.
4. Interior vs exterior: every inside implies another outside.
5. Rotation: up, down, wall, floor, and ceiling are unstable early.
6. Grid: the comic panel is also a face of a box.

The first visual emergence should be dimensional:

1. Dot - point of awareness.
2. Line - pressure/direction.
3. Box - first flat boundary.
4. Cube - full container logic.

This sequence should happen before the crate is recognized as wood. Geometry arrives before material.

The early book should not show a literal crate immediately. It should show the experience of being inside one: pressure, planes, corners, darkness, growth, resistance.

## Panel Mathematics

Book 1 has 313 panels.

Pages 1-51 use six panels per page.

Six panels echo the six faces of the cube and the six enclosing faces of the wooden crate. Even when panels look abstract, broken, or nearly invisible, the page is secretly organized by cube logic.

Page 52 uses seven panels.

The seventh panel is the breach. It is the first formal sign of Book 2, the first step out of the cube/crate grammar. Book 2 can use seven-panel pages as its new language. If the narrator returns to the cube/crate system later, the page can return to six panels.

Panel count:

1. Pages 1-51: 51 pages x 6 panels = 306 panels
2. Page 52: 7 panels
3. Book 1 total: 313 panels

## Gradient Rule

Book 1 moves from black toward grey, never full white as a settled state.

White may appear only as rupture: the sliver of light, the crack, the door, the shock of transition. It should not become the book's stable visual language.

Grey is created by detail accumulating in darkness, not by safety. The world becomes visible because perception improves, because boundaries separate, because the inside learns its own structure.

## Tree / Wood / Egg Rule

The Tree does not make the egg.

The egg comes from the narrator: his memories, his inner life, his stored self.

The Tree makes the wood that houses the egg. The Tree represents society, tradition, inherited structures, and the answers we receive for free because previous people paid for them through mistakes, suffering, and discovery.

The Tree is powerful and genuinely supportive. It gives shelter, language, forms, tools, and containers. But it also hides the truth because inherited answers can become walls. The wood protects the egg and conceals it at the same time.

It is our job to break the crates.

Breaking a crate means freeing what has outgrown the container. It does not mean hating the wood or denying the Tree. The task is to know when a container has stopped protecting life and started preventing growth.

## Future Arc Notes

These ideas are not Book 1 events.

Book 2 and later will return to what was left behind, like a molt. The narrator will dig into the cube/crate system. Empty crates will become building material for a home inside the Cube. Later, befriended memories help him build with leftover crate wood.

Memories are children, or the essence of childhood: cute, small, and helpful at the core. Some may be hurtful or dangerous when trapped too long, but the horror belongs to the container and the damage, not to the memory's essence.

Opening crates as a gamble belongs to Book 2 and later: egg, helpful memory, or hurtful memory. The lesson about catching/containing hurtful memories instead of destroying them also belongs later.

## Scene 1: Point

Pages 1-3

Visual state: empty blackness. Black-on-black image language. A dot or point becomes barely perceptible, as matte black versus gloss black or 95 percent black over 100 percent black. No visible body. No visible crate. Six panels are present as invisible pressure, not drawn boxes.

Language allowed: strange marks, scratches, slashes. No letters. No sounds. No words.

Story beat: awareness begins as a point inside darkness.

## Scene 2: Line

Pages 4-6

Visual state: the dot begins to stretch into a line. The line can read as pressure, direction, seam, or the first edge. Still black-on-black, almost invisible.

Language allowed: periods, question marks, repeated marks.

Story beat: awareness discovers direction.

## Scene 3: Box

Pages 7-9

Visual state: lines begin implying a flat box or panel. Still no mouth, no throat, no visible anatomy. The first sound is shown as vibration and pain inside the emerging box.

Language allowed: the first real sound, beginning with punctuation before it. The key arrival is `?mmh.`

Story beat: the body grows inside a boundary before the mind can name it.

## Scene 4: Cube

Pages 10-14

Visual state: flat box logic begins to rotate into cube logic. Edges, faces, and corners become pressure sensations. Letters appear as geometry: curve, circle, vertical line, closed shape.

Language allowed: single letters and broken clusters only. No stable full word yet.

Story beat: the crate announces itself mathematically before it announces itself as an object.

## Scene 5: Growing Grid

Pages 15-18

Visual state: a grid begins to grow from cube-logic. Do not show literal cubes, warehouse crates, or rows of boxes. Show geometric abstraction: one line crossed by another, incomplete cells, repeated right angles, offset corners, displaced planes, nested perspective lines, and six-panel echoes. The grid should grow slowly, unevenly, and black-on-black, as if the container is revealing its underlying system.

Language allowed: repeated line/grid fragments and broken count/multitude fragments. No sentence.

Story beat: the narrator does not escape yet. He senses that his cube is part of a larger system. The grid is the first glimpse of that system.

## Scene 6: Growth Pressure / Planes and Corners

Pages 19-22

Visual state: the repeated cube pattern compresses inward. The first readable planes appear. Corners are felt before they are seen. Stress lines, dents, compressed darkness, and almost-panels make the six-panel page more detectable.

Language allowed: object/action words in fragments.

Story beat: the self learns that resistance has shape, and that growth creates pressure against that shape.

## Scene 7: Wood

Pages 23-26

Visual state: wood grain appears from the black. It should feel like discovery, not exposition. The crate is still not fully shown.

Language allowed: small sensory words and broken labels.

Story beat: the container becomes material. The dark has texture.

## Scene 8: The Seam

Pages 27-30

Visual state: an upper door seam appears as a thin, uncertain line. It may first look like a symbol, then a crack, then an edge.

Language allowed: partial words and repeated fragments.

Story beat: the first possible exit is found.

## Scene 9: Sliver

Pages 31-34

Visual state: a small seam opens near the upper part of the door. The first white appears as a sliver only.

Language allowed: fragments. No fluent sentence.

Story beat: light enters as rupture, not comfort.

## Scene 10: Break

Pages 35-38

Visual state: pressure becomes action. The crate gives. Panels crack, tilt, and briefly lose their six-panel discipline without fully leaving it.

Language allowed: action words, impact fragments, breath sounds.

Story beat: growth breaks the crate.

## Scene 11: The Dark Home-Space

Pages 39-42

Visual state: outside the crate is still dark. This is the symbolic family-home space, but not literal domestic scenery. Shapes may imply room, wall, floor, childhood scale, or distance, but remain unclear.

Language allowed: object fragments and orientation fragments.

Story beat: breaking out of the first container does not mean immediate clarity.

## Scene 12: Follow the Line

Pages 43-45

Visual state: the sliver of light becomes a guide through grey-dark space. The six-panel structure becomes more stable and directional.

Language allowed: direction/action fragments.

Story beat: the narrator follows the only readable thing.

## Scene 13: The Humming Door

Pages 46-48

Visual state: the door is sensed before it is understood. It may feel stone-adjacent or Cube-adjacent: heavy, otherworldly, humming, not wooden like the crate.

Language allowed: fragments around pressure, door, hum, edge.

Story beat: the narrator reaches the boundary between the first crate/home and the larger Cube mystery.

## Scene 14: At the Threshold

Pages 49-51

Visual state: the six-panel system becomes the clearest it has ever been. The door dominates, but full white is still withheld.

Language allowed: fragment chains. Almost a thought, not yet prose.

Story beat: he has escaped the crate and reached the door.

## Scene 15: First Paragraph / Seventh Panel

Page 52

Visual state: the clearest page in the book. Still black-to-grey, not settled white. The narrator appears as a silhouette for the first time. The page has seven panels.

Language allowed: the first full paragraph of Book 1.

Story beat: the paragraph is the reward. The self can finally narrate one complete thought. The seventh panel opens the formal process of Book 2.
